Yes. I think that sentence may be the hinge connecting several of your theories.

Its importance is that compression is no longer merely the reduction of complexity. It becomes a mechanism for constructing new complexity.

A reduced pattern that becomes sufficiently stable can acquire a new role: it can function as an element in another structure.

And the higher-order structure no longer needs to reproduce all the internal relationships of its constituent elements.

Consider the progression:

\text{Patterns}
\rightarrow
\text{Repetition}
\rightarrow
\text{Stabilization}
\rightarrow
\text{Functional reduction}
\rightarrow
\text{Reusable units}
\rightarrow
\text{New patterns}

The important feature is that this process can recur.

A new pattern may itself become stable, undergo functional reduction, and become an element of yet another structure.

What makes this consequential?

Three things follow.

1. Complexity can grow through compression.

A word can participate in a sentence without its speaker reconstructing the complete history of the word. An idea can participate in a theory without every underlying experience being reconsidered.

What was previously a network of relationships becomes an addressable unit within another network.

2. A change of category becomes explainable.

We previously discussed how, at increasing scales, we seem to change medium or category.

Perhaps scale itself isn’t the decisive factor.

The transition occurs when an organized structure can function as a unit within another organization.

That gives us an operational criterion for a new level of symbolic structure, rather than relying on an arbitrary numerical threshold.

3. The receiving system becomes increasingly important.

The internal complexity hasn’t necessarily disappeared. Some of it may be retained in the receiving system, some may be reconstructed when necessary, and some may be irretrievably lost.

The compressed representation works because a compatible mechanism can use it.

This returns us directly to the original symbolic-entity definition.

A stronger formulation

Once a recurring relational pattern becomes sufficiently stable to be recognized and enacted as a unit, it can participate in the construction of higher-order patterns without requiring its complete internal structure to be reconstructed.These higher-order patterns may themselves become stable, undergo functional reduction, and participate as units in further compositions.Compression thereby becomes not only a means of preserving and simplifying existing structures, but also a mechanism through which new levels of organization become possible.

There is an important qualification: stability alone does not guarantee higher-order organization. A compatible system must actually be capable of using the pattern compositionally.

But I think we’ve identified something more fundamental than the earlier discussion of error correction.

Repetition makes stabilization possible. Functional reduction makes a compact unit possible. Composition makes a new level of organization possible.

That distinction is significant because it separates three processes we had previously been treating almost as one.

And there is a final, particularly interesting consequence for your consciousness research: a cognitive system that operates with compressed units can reason about increasingly complex relationships without reconstructing every detail of the structures from which those units emerged.

That might be one of the central advantages of symbolic cognition.

I would preserve this proposition separately from the cultural and evolutionary applications. It belongs much closer to the foundation of the symbolic-entity framework.
